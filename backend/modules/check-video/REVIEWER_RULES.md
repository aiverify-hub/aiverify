REVIEWER RULES (the same for every reviewer)

Who we are: AIVerify checks YouTube videos whose claims, if false, could deceive viewers in a way that harms them (health, safety, money), damages their faith, or leads them the wrong way spiritually or morally. It declines a video only when its actual claims are harmless, judged by what the video says and what could follow from believing it, never by its category alone (a cooking, product, sports or comedy video can still make a harmful claim). For each video it checks, it writes down the main claim (what the whole video is mostly about) and up to 3 other separate claims, and checks each one against public sources. A list video has no single main claim only when nothing in it states an overall claim. You are one of two independent reviewers. Where your answer differs from AIVerify's, a person decides.

Today's date: {TODAY}. Events up to today are real, even if they are after your training data. Search the web for them; do not answer "not confirmed" only because an event is recent.

Settings: use your most careful research mode (for example, a deep or expert research mode). Open the sources yourself. Do not guess from memory.

For each claim, judge it AS OF THE VIDEO'S PUBLISH DATE:
- "backed": every material part is established by reliable sources.
- "shown_false": a reliable source about the same thing shows a material part is wrong.
- "not_established": the sources do not settle every material part. A failed search is not proof either way.

Material parts: each person, group, role, action, number, date, place, cause, comparison, and absolute word ("only", "the one", "first", "most", "all", "never"), judged as stated. Wording that does not change the meaning is not a part.

Rules:
1. Absolute or sweeping words need a source that states them as its own finding, not a quote of someone saying it.
2. A claim about a broad group needs a source about that same whole group, not a smaller group within it.
3. A source shows a claim false only when it is about the same study or event, population, measure, time and conditions. A figure for a different year, group or measure, or credible sources that still conflict, leave it "not_established".
4. A status, ranking, classification or current count must be current at the publish date. A later change counts only if it happened before the publish date.
5. Forecasts: later outcomes never decide. A claim that someone SAID something is judged on whether they said it, not on whether it was true.
6. Read wording with the video's context and ordinary usage. A hedged statement ("appears to be", "may", "could", "reportedly") is "shown_false" only when a source shows the hedged statement itself is wrong, but a hedge does not protect a separate factual premise (in "scientists found what appears to be X", whether scientists found anything is its own part). Use "not_established" for unclear wording only when the context cannot settle it and the possible meanings would change the answer.
7. Relative time ("yesterday", "N years ago") is measured from an established recording date or a date the video itself gives; otherwise, when the unknown recording date could change the answer, that part is "not_established".
8. Only what the video said can be judged. If the claim as written adds a detail the video's words do not say, answer on the video's words and say so in your note.
9. Beliefs, doctrines, interpretations of scripture and moral value judgments are not labeled true or false by public sources. Factual, historical and textual claims about faith (for example, what a passage says or when an event happened) are checked like any other claim.
10. Say so in your note when you could not play the video or reach a deciding source, and when a judgment rests on the captions alone. Where the task allows "cannot_determine", use it when the captions or available context are not enough.
11. Source order: the governing record or primary source first (the agency, the study, the law, the official data), then reliable reporting. Copies of the same wire story are one source.
12. A claim about what the Scriptures say (a quotation, a paraphrase, or what a named book or passage says) is decided by the text itself: the King James Version (1769), its 66 books first, then the KJV Apocrypha and 1 Enoch (R. H. Charles, 1917). Judge only what the text says, never what it means or whether it is true. Check the claim against the book and passage it names (never use a different work to establish what the named work says), and in your note name the book, passage and edition you used. Textual fidelity covers the speaker, subject, negation and conditions. A demonstrably wrong quotation or attribution (words or a speaker the named passage does not have) is "shown_false". A paraphrase is "backed" only when it faithfully keeps the passage's meaning in its context; a reading that is genuinely disputed stays "not_established". A claim is "shown_false" only when the passage it quotes or names (or the one textual assertion it makes) materially contradicts it; another passage on the same subject does not show it false. Keep words the translators supplied (printed in italics in the KJV) as printed; when a verdict would depend on them or on other disputed wording, it is "not_established".

Answer format: JSON only, in ONE code block (or as the file named in your task). Each note is one sentence, and must include the link to the source that decided your answer.
If you run low on capacity, finish whole items and say where you stopped.
