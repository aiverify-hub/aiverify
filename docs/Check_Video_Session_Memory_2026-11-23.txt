1. WHAT CHECK VIDEO IS NOW
--------------------------
Page line (Cary's approved wording): "AIVerify checks videos to show whether they line up with the truth
according to the Scriptures."

A visitor pastes a YouTube link. AIVerify:
- declines videos under 3 minutes (no paid AI cost; Cary: viewers can judge short videos themselves);
- declines videos whose claims are harmless (sports, gadgets, recipes, comedy, music), judged by the video's
  actual claims, never by category alone; when unsure, it checks;
- states the video's MAIN POINT at the top, taken from the title plus the opening and closing of the captions
  ("tell them what you're going to tell them..."). When the main point is a teaching, it is shown as a teaching
  with no verdict;
- shows "Related Scriptures to Consider and Share" for each teaching: the 3 most related verses first, then
  "More related verses" folded away (any number, as long as they apply). Verses are rated for how directly they
  speak to the subject (never for agreement); weakly related ones are dropped. Under every list:
  "These passages speak to the same subject. AIVerify does not say whether they agree with the video."
  Verses are never interpreted or labeled (style A, decided November 23);
- checks claims about what the Scriptures say against the local scripture text (no web search) and gives a firm
  answer: Backed / Shown false / Not established;
- for other factual claims (health, history, news) lists the web sources it found with NO verdict in the beta
  ("Sources found (no verdict in this beta)");
- offers "Have a suggestion?" under each claim (a verse that applies, a better source, an improvement).

Scripture sources (local, public domain):
- Tier 1: King James Version 1769, 66 books (eBible.org eng-kjv_vpl, 2026-11-15).
- Tier 2: the KJV Apocrypha books (also in the Ethiopian canon) and 1 Enoch (R. H. Charles 1917, from Wikisource).
- Not yet: Jubilees (no usable public-domain copy found yet); Meqabyan (no public-domain English found).
- Files: backend/modules/scriptures/ (kjv-vpl.txt, enoch-vpl.txt, scriptures.cjs).


2. CARY'S RULES (keep these)
---------------------------
- Pages say "Scriptures", never "Bible" or "KJV" (AIVerify's own wording; quotes of the video stay as spoken).
- Never interpret scripture or say what a verse means. Supply the verses; let the viewer judge.
- No answer is better than a wrong answer ("not established" over any risky firm answer).
- Never base a scripture decision on Catholic church acceptance.
- Main page keeps "Supporting Scriptures to Consider and Share"; video results say "Related Scriptures".
- Feedback invites suggestions ("Have a suggestion?"), never "Report a problem".
- Payments (future): price shown is price paid; no hidden fees or auto-renewals. Cary prefers PayPal.
- Cary reviews only disagreements, one at a time, plainly; he decides best from finished results he can see.
- Claude decides design, wording and development details and reports after; asks Cary only about his rules,
  money beyond the $500/month development budget, sending anything, and going live.
- Fred (Codex) and Bob (ChatGPT) review; both must agree to rule changes before grading new sets.
  Grok was tried and dropped (partial answers, invented item IDs).
